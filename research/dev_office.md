## Room language

A real developer's home office is a spare bedroom corner, not a showroom. The desk sits against the back
wall with side light from a casement window, a white panel radiator under the window and a deep window
board where the cat sleeps. The wall above the desk holds a white SKÅDIS pegboard for headphones and small
things, and a cork notice board with sticky notes. Storage is white IKEA flat-pack: an ALEX drawer unit
beside the desk and a KALLAX with fabric boxes. On the desk: two 24-inch monitors on a clamp arm, a
tenkeyless mechanical keyboard on a felt desk mat, a mouse, a mug, a closed laptop and a clamp-on work
lamp. Materials are honest: oak veneer, white melamine, powder-coated steel, cork, felt, glazed ceramic and
plastered walls with a painted skirting board. The cosy layer comes from the Monstera, a striped flatweave
rug, warm lamp light and books.

Sources consulted for the setup language: https://www.makerstations.io/pegboard-desk-setup-ideas ,
https://www.coohom.com/article/dev-room-design-smart-ideas-for-coding-spaces

## Review pass (art-director fixes)

What still looked generic after the first rebuild, and what changed:

1. **Task chair**: the back was a thin oval loop with a see-through, noisy panel and a bar across it, so it looked like a tennis racket. It is now
   a real mesh-chair back: narrow at the waist and wide at the shoulders, with a lumbar curve, sides that wrap forward, a thick moulded frame,
   a denser mesh, and a U-shaped yoke from the tilt mechanism. The armrests now sit on curved supports with soft pads.
2. **Monstera**: the leaves had many hair-thin slits and pointed up like a banana or palm. They are now broad hearts with two lobes behind the
   stalk, 4 wider splits per side that follow the side veins, and a shallow V-fold along the midrib. They hang outward and down from long
   arching stalks.
3. **Empty front of the room**: the front-right third of the floor was bare boards. It is now a reading corner: a POÄNG bentwood armchair,
   which is instantly recognisable as IKEA, with the knitted pouffe as its footstool.
4. **Pouffe**: it was a plain cylinder. It now has chunky cable-knit ribs.
5. **Dual monitors**: the two screens were flat in one plane. Each is now turned in 9°, the way real dual setups are arranged. The screen
   hooks turn with them.
6. **PC tower**: it read as an empty white box, because the alpha glass rendered as grey noise and the fans faced away from the camera. It now
   has a smoked glass edge, a PSU shroud, a graphics-card backplate, a CPU cooler with a lit top, a rear exhaust fan with a lit ring, and lit RAM.
