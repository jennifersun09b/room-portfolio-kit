# Fairytale Atelier: real-world references

Room: `templates/fairytale_atelier.py` (themes `morning_blush`, `midnight_ball`). This is a gown designer's dressing salon with a storybook, princesscore / royalcore feeling. It is built as a real working couture atelier, so it also suits fashion, costume, bridal and styling portfolios. Product names appear only in this file and in code comments. The scene has no logos, brand names, franchise names or trademarked graphics.

## Venue and room language

A bridal or evening-wear boutique fitting salon (independent bridal boutiques, couture ateliers, prom and occasion-wear shops) has:

- painted panelled walls (dado rail, tall upper panels picked out with a fine gilt line), a cornice and skirting;
- a pale oak herringbone or chevron parquet with a border, and a large round rug in the fitting area;
- a round upholstered **fitting podium** in the middle, so the bride can see the hem in the mirrors;
- a **freestanding gown rail** in polished brass, often curved, with gowns face-out on satin padded hangers;
- a **full-length leaner mirror** with a gilt arched frame;
- a **dressing table** with a lit "Hollywood" bulb mirror and a tufted stool;
- a glass-topped **pedestal vitrine** for tiaras, veils and jewellery;
- a crystal **candle chandelier** over the podium;
- in a working atelier, a **dress form** on a cast-iron caster base, a domestic sewing machine on a small table by the window, a sketch easel with croquis and a fabric swatch board;
- **shoe ledges** with satin court shoes, round **hat boxes** and a boutique shopping bag with tissue.

## Notes for modelling
- Rail bar 2.0 m (bridal rails run 1.8-2.0 m so long trains clear the floor), dressing table 76 cm, stool seat 46 cm, podium 15 cm, window sill 0.92 m, chandelier hung from 2.15 m to 3.1 m, about 0.5 m above the dress form's neck.
- Thin parts stay thin: table tops 25 mm, shelves 22 mm, hanger hooks 5-6 mm, chair and stool legs 25-30 mm.
- Bevels on hard parts are 2-8 mm. Only cushions, the pouf, the podium top and the stool top use soft bevels.
- All wood, fabric, metal, paper and plaster use `surface()` from `tools/snippets/surfaces.py`, so themes keep working.

## IP-safety
The room only evokes a genre: the princesscore / royalcore "royal dressing room" feeling. Everything in it is a real boutique object or an original design. These are the rules we followed (described generically on purpose, so this file does not itself list any franchise's designs):
- **No franchise, studio or character names** anywhere: scene, object names, comments, file names, theme names or tags. The file is `fairytale_atelier` and the themes are `morning_blush` and `midnight_ball`.
- **No recreated costume designs.** None of the gowns copies a well-known film or character costume, or the colour scheme of one. Each gown pairs one of six different silhouettes (mermaid with straps, cap-sleeve A-line with a sash, tiered tulle ball skirt, off-shoulder A-line, empire halter, slip column) with one gemstone or flower colour (Moonstone, Sage, Peony, Opal, Midnight, Champagne) from a seasonal pastel palette. No gown combines the colour, neckline and sleeves of a recognisable costume.
- **No story-specific props.** Every prop is an ordinary item from a real bridal boutique or atelier: satin court shoes in coloured fabric on a shop shelf, round hat boxes, a dress form, a sewing machine, a bulb mirror, a plain gilt floor mirror, a candle chandelier. There are no props that belong to one particular tale, no talking or animal helpers, no carriages, no castle silhouettes, crests, house names or heraldry.
- **The tiara is an original** floral band of seven rising sprays with crystals and pearls, not a copy of any film tiara.
- **No quotes or slogans.** Visible text is only the user's name and role, "~ ATELIER ~", and the project labels.
- **No brand marks** on the products we based pieces on (bulb mirror, dress form, sewing machine, chandelier). Product names appear only in comments and in this file.
