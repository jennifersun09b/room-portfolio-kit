# Animal City Loft: real-world references

Room: `templates/animal_city_apartment.py` (themes `sunny_downtown`, `neon_nightlife`). It is a sunny corner loft in a big, friendly city where residents of every size live side by side. The room evokes the "mixed-scale animal metropolis" feeling (bright urban colour blocking, small-and-tall jokes, a city of many climates outside the window, a cosy hobby-mystery corner) with entirely original designs.

Signature: the three doors for three sizes, and the climate-district skyline in the big factory window. Product names appear only in this file and in code comments. The scene has no logos, brand names, franchise names or trademarked graphics.

Who it is for: animators and character designers, children's illustrators, toy and plush makers, pet businesses (groomers, sitters, vets), kids' educators, mystery and true-crime podcasters (the case board), and families.

## Room language

A converted warehouse loft or city apartment has:

- white plaster walls with a **colour-blocked painted dado** under an oak chair rail, and 100 mm skirting;
- wide **oak floorboards** in random lengths;
- a big **steel factory window** (W20-type, 4 x 4 panes, 2.35 x 2.25 m) with a deep oak window board;
- panelled internal doors with architraves, brass lever handles, kick plates and a coir doormat.

The "city built for every size" idea comes from scale cues, each based on a real object at its real size:

- an oversized arched pivot door (2.95 m) next to a standard 826 × 2040 mm door, under an "ALL SIZES WELCOME" fascia sign;
- a 17 × 25 cm pet-flap-sized door in the skirting, with its own orange welcome mat with a paw print, a red-and-cream striped shop awning, a glowing porch lamp, a house number and a post mailbox;
- a painted height chart on the tall door's frame, with paw-print tags that grow with each height;
- a 1:12 dollhouse-scale café and market built into the skirting;
- a hat-and-coat board with hooks at three heights and a hat shelf at giraffe height;
- stools in three sizes (25, 65 and 105 cm), cups in three sizes (6 cl, 35 cl, 3 l), a tiny tub chair beside the full-size one, and a step stool;
- an extra-tall reading lamp arching over the desk corner.

## The city outside the window

The skyline is a shallow light box behind the window (no shadows, so the sun still shines in). The camera sees a point `d` behind the glass `d` metres further back and `0.88 d` lower, so every piece is placed in window coordinates and then pushed back. The lid of the box slopes down towards the back so it never shows above the wall.

The city is made of four districts with different climates side by side, one per window column, so the mullions frame each one. All the architecture is original and built from real building types:

- **Snow district:** two ranges of peaks with ragged snow caps, three hexagonal ice-crystal towers with frosted floor bands and pointed tips, snow-roofed chalets, frosted pines and light snowfall.
- **Desert quarter:** sand dunes, a stepped adobe mesa block with rounded parapets, small windows and wooden beam ends (Pueblo-style vigas), a slim wind tower with vent slots (a Persian windcatcher type), a small yellow dome, date palms, and the sun.
- **Downtown:** a teal glass tower with a window grid and fins carrying the billboard (your hero project, `screen` hook 1, centred in a pane as the camera sees it), a blue stepped deco tower with coping, piers and a lit spire, and hazy towers behind.
- **Rainforest dome:** a green glass geodesic biome dome (Eden-Project type) with teal struts and trees inside, giant rainforest trees with hanging vines behind it, drifting mist, and a tree-planted tower behind.

In front runs a row of rooftops, one per district (snow, sand, brick with a water tank, a roof garden), with a string of lights. By day the sky has clouds and a sun over the desert; at night it has a moon, stars, lit windows, glowing ice towers and a lit dome.

## Notes for modelling

- All sizes are real-world metres. The room is 5.0 × 4.4 m with 3.3 m walls (a tall loft, so the 2.95 m door fits).
- Construction detail on the new pieces: butcher-block staves, 3 mm drawer and door gaps, panelled door, casters with forks, footrings, rubber feet, a hand hole in the step stool, bamboo nodes and twigs, a gilt frame with a seal and ribbons, a light bar and hubcaps on the toy car.
- Construction detail: drawer gaps of 3 mm or more; bar pulls on posts; tapered legs; casters with forks; a spindle back; piping; a recessed plinth; architraves and kick plates; brass collars and clamps on the tube; felt bands on the capsules.
- Materials: `surface()` kinds used are wood (oak, butcher block, step stool), veneer (floorboards), fabric (upholstery, straw hat, tea towel), knit (plush, throws, paw rug, carrot cushion, coir), leather (suitcases, phone case), brushed (window steel, brass, gilt frame, badge, stool steel), terrazzo (cork granules), marble (lamp base, café tables), ceramic (radio, kettle, toaster, cups, fruit, bamboo pot, toy car), paper, plaster (paint) and concrete (terracotta, pavement).

## IP-safety

The room pushes a genre as far as it goes, not a film: a friendly modern city where animals of every size live side by side. Everything in it is an original design, and every prop is a generic object that appears in many homes and many stories.

- **Names and text:** no film, franchise, city, district or character names anywhere: not in the scene, object names, comments, file names or this file. Every label is generic and original ("ALL SIZES WELCOME", "CERTIFICATE", "PATROL TRAINING", "CITY TRANSIT", "THE DAILY PAW", "CAFE", "MARKET", "MAIL", "HELLO, CITY!"). No quotes or catchphrases.
- **Characters:** no characters and no lookalikes of any existing animated character, species pairing, outfit or uniform. The residents are simple rounded knit plush toys (otter, raccoon, meerkat, tortoise) with bead eyes and no clothing. There are no posters or pictures of characters.
- **Mixed-scale cues:** the size jokes (doors, stools, cups, chairs, hooks, a height chart, a mouse mailbox) are a common genre device in many picture books, films and toys; each piece here is built from a real product at its real size.
- **City:** the climate districts are a broad idea; the room uses it only as one colour-coded district per window pane, with original architecture built from real building types (ice-crystal towers, an adobe mesa block, a windcatcher tower, a deco tower, a glass tower, a geodesic biome dome). There are no recognisable film landmarks, no named districts, no dividing climate walls, no map of any film's city and no film vehicles or trains. The transit map and the phone map are original diagrams.
- **Patrol props:** the certificate, the star badge and the toy car are generic: a plain five-point star (a shape used by many real and toy badges), a two-tone toy car with a red-and-blue light bar, and a certificate with only generic words. No department name, crest, emblem, number, shield shape or logo, and no film gadgets.
- **Food and cushions:** carrots appear only as everyday food (a few in the fruit bowl) and as a common food-shaped cushion; the rest of the produce is apples, citrus, greens and a cucumber. There are no props that exist only in one franchise.
- **No implied protagonist:** the room is for every size of resident, so it avoids stacking cues that together would point at one particular character's species, hometown or job story. The newspaper is "THE DAILY PAW" (all paws, not one species), the bed is a hill nook rather than a burrow, produce is mixed, the phone case is teal, and the patrol props are a hobby shelf among many other interests (case board, radio, transit map).
- **Colours and layout:** the palette is the room's own colour blocking (teal, citrus orange, sunflower, lime); no franchise's signature colour pairing, logo typography or real film-set layout is reproduced.
- **Marks:** no logos, brand names or trademarked graphics. Product names appear only in code comments and in this file.

## Art-direction review (render passes)

1. **Pass 1.** The juice cart sat at the front corner, closest to the camera, and its canopy covered the tub chair. The pet-scale door was hidden behind the hill hood.
   - Fix: moved the cart to the foot of the bed and lowered the canopy to 1.42 m. Re-laid the back wall as tall door, pet door, standard door, then hook board.
2. **Pass 2.** The tall lamp's shade read as a second desk lamp in front of the desk. The case-board photos were too small. The pet door was too tiny to read at 1080p. Two towers were mostly outside the window.
   - Fix: moved the lamp to the window-side corner. Enlarged the photos to 36 × 26 cm and the pet door to 17 × 25 cm with a cream frame. Shifted the towers into the visible band. Painted the cart frame.
3. **Pass 3.** Toned down the window glints for the night theme, brought the two right-hand towers forward of the glass tower, and darkened and enlarged the newspaper's role line for contrast.
5. **Independent review pass.** IP: the skyline's snow-capped, desert and jungle towers read together as themed climate districts, which is too close to one specific film city. They became an eclectic mix of real architectural types: a domed sandstone tower, a stepped deco tower with coping and piers (no snow or icicles) and a vertical-forest tower (no waterfall). The colour keys were renamed to match, and the avoid-list in this file was rewritten so that it no longer describes any franchise's characters or props. Art: the juice cart's flat canopy read as a striped table, and it stood right in front of the bed foot.
   - Fix: rebuilt the cart with a peaked awning on four poles, a scalloped valance all round, apron rails, thicker uprights and a larger framed "FRESH JUICE" sign. Moved it into the empty front-left floor, turned towards the camera, and moved the tub chair back on the rug so the awning doesn't cover it.
   - Fix: added a stack of suitcases in three sizes at the foot of the bed, a bed-end table that also carries the "every size" joke.
   - Fix: enlarged the newspaper front page by 18 % and set the role line in red, so name and role read at hero size.
   - Fix: softened and shortened the window glints, which looked like scratches by day, and made the grass and flowers on the hill hood much denser.
6. **Closer-vibe pass (genre push).** Goal: make the room feel as much like a mixed-scale animal metropolis as possible while staying original.
   - Added: a bigger 4 x 4 pane window and a skyline of four climate districts (snow, desert, downtown, rainforest dome); a kitchen island with a kettle, a toaster, fruit and carrots; stools, cups and chairs in three sizes and a step stool; a hat shelf at giraffe height; a height chart on the tall door frame; a mouse mailbox and house number; an "ALL SIZES WELCOME" sign; a framed patrol-training certificate, a gold star badge and a toy patrol car; a smartphone with a city map on the desk; a paw rug, a bamboo plant and a carrot cushion.    - Round 1: the skyline's near rooftops filled a third of the window; the mountains were too pale; the desert read as orange blocks; the dome was faint; the step stool read side-on like an easel; the carrot cushion was hidden under the hood; the tall lamp covered the window's edge. 7. **Independent IP and art review (closer-vibe pass, round 4).**
   - IP: each prop was generic, but several cues stacked up towards one specific animated protagonist's back story (a burrow-named newspaper and bed, carrots in four places, an orange phone, patrol-training props).
