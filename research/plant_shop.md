# Plant Shop: real-world references

Room: `templates/plant_shop.py` (themes `morning`, `golden_hour`). This is an independent London plant shop and florist of the kind run by Conservatory Archives (Hackney / Clapton), Prick (Dalston), Grow London or Bloomist. Brand and product names appear only in this file and in code comments. The scene has no logos, brand names or trademarked graphics.

## Venue and room language

A small independent plant shop is usually a converted shopfront or railway-arch unit. It has painted (white or off-white) London stock brick walls, a big steel-framed shop window with slim glazing bars, and a plain polished-concrete or screed floor with saw-cut joints. Little in it is bespoke joinery. The shop is filled with things you can recognise: galvanised steel shelving units packed with potted plants (often IKEA HYLLIS-style), sometimes with LED grow-light bars under the shelves; a stepped wooden stand of galvanised florist buckets holding cut stems (tulips, roses, eucalyptus) under the window; and an old shop counter or a timber counter with a tongue-and-groove front, a tablet till, a small card reader and a roll of kraft wrapping paper on a steel cutter. Plants are sold in terracotta, in classic tapered "standard" pots and long toms, and in glazed ceramic pots, and the big floor plants (fiddle-leaf fig, Strelitzia, olive tree, monstera) sit in nursery pots inside bigger planters. A potting bench with a metal-lined top, a top shelf, tool hooks and a soil bin is where repotting happens. Macramé hangers with trailing pothos hang on a rail in the window. Pendant grow lights hang over the display table. Botanical prints in cheap black frames go on the walls, and a chalk A-board stands on the pavement or just inside the door. Prick is known for a minimal white space with wooden shelves and its own terracotta pots, and Conservatory Archives for dense, jungle-like displays with vintage furniture.

## Review notes (second pass)

- Monstera leaves are now heart-shaped blades with forward-swept slits that stop short of the midrib and a row of small fenestration holes beside it (the "Swiss cheese" look). Strelitzia's older leaves tear right to the midrib instead, as they do in real life.
- The painted brick shows only a faint brick-to-brick difference with slightly shadowed mortar, the way one coat of masonry paint looks, not the two-tone pattern of bare brick.
- The street behind the shop window (paving, kerb, tarmac, a tree pit) is cut to the band of the picture that the window covers, so it never shows past the end of the back wall.
