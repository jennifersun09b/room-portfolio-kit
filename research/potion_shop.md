# Apothecary / Herb Shop: real-world references

Room: `templates/potion_shop.py` (themes `moonlit`, `harvest`). The room is a real apothecary and herb shop of the kind you can still visit. It combines a Victorian chemist's shop (dispensing cabinets, brass balances, labelled drawers), a modern occult or tarot bookshop (Treadwell's and The Atlantis Bookshop in London), the Herb Garret at the Old Operating Theatre Museum (dried herbs and an apothecary's workroom) and an Etsy-style candle studio. There is no film or game IP. Brand and shop names appear only in this file and in code comments. The scene has no logos or trademarked graphics.

## Venue and room language

A real apothecary is a working shop built from joinery. One wall is a floor-to-cornice dispensing cabinet in walnut or oak. Its lower half is a bank of small drawers, each with a brass card-frame label holder above a cup pull and a hand-written paper label. Above a worktop, a shallower hutch has more small drawers and then open shelves of glass shop rounds with ground-glass stoppers, amber Boston-round bottles with corks, cobalt poison bottles and two-tone stoneware crocks, all with paper labels.

Customers stand at a panelled mahogany shop counter. It is about 91 cm high with a polished top and a plinth, and holds a brass-and-mahogany balance with its weights, a service bell and trays of tumbled stones. A brass rail across the cabinet carries a hook-on library ladder. Bundles of lavender, sage and rosemary hang head-down from a pulley clothes airer (a Sheila Maid) with cast-iron ends and pine laths, raised on a rope to a cleat on the wall.

The walls are painted tongue-and-groove (beadboard) panelling to dado height with dark limewash plaster above. Framed botanical prints with cream mounts and a chalkboard of the day's blends hang on the walls. Brass swan-neck wall lights carry small prismatic glass shades. The floor is laid with Victorian quarry or encaustic tiles set diagonally in two colours.

The witchy layer is real retail: a cast-iron cauldron hung from a camp tripod as a display piece, church pillar candles with real drips on brass plates, a glazed oak display cabinet of amethyst, quartz and selenite, a reading corner with a wing chair and a small pedestal table under a fringed velvet cloth with a tarot spread, a birch besom broom, a Turkish kilim and a black shop cat.

## Notes for modelling
- Counter 92 cm, chair seat 45 cm, window sill on the dado at 1.05 m, ladder rail 2.2 m, cabinet worktop 0.92 m.
- Thin parts stay thin: shelves 20 mm, counter top 35 mm, drawer fronts 18 mm, ladder stiles 25 × 60 mm, iron rods 22 mm.
- Bevels are 2-8 mm on hard parts. Soft bevels are used only on the chair cushions.
- Glowing colour comes only from real light sources: candles, wall lights, the window, and cork-LED string lights (a coil of warm-white LEDs) in a few deep-coloured glass bottles (a common Etsy decoration). The brew in the cauldron is a dark liquid with only a soft glow.
- Shop jars hold real dried-herb colours (chamomile, rose buds, nettle, lavender, calendula, hibiscus, roots) with a chip texture so they read as dried flowers, not paint.
- The display cabinet uses clear float glass (green edges, brass shelf studs) and a neutral 3600 K light.
